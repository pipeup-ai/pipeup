Pipeup in the browser, not on the page

With the extension, everything you use Pipeup with lives in the browser itself: its menu in the toolbar panel, and the comments, where you read, reply and write, in the browser's side panel. The page keeps only what has to be on it: the highlights that say where comments are, and the outline that shows what you're about to comment on.

4. What Pipeup needs for this

Pipeup today draws its menu, panel, threads and comment box itself, inside the page. For the extension to draw them instead, Pipeup gains one way of running and a few things a host can ask and hear (an addition to §4a):

  • Drawn by the host: Pipeup draws only highlights, pins, comment mode's outline and hint on the page; it opens no thread, panel or box there.
  • It tells the host when a block is chosen in comment mode (with what it's on), when a highlight is clicked, and when the comments change.
  • The host can go to a thread (scroll to it and mark its highlight), and write, reply, react, resolve, edit and delete through the document Pipeup already exposes.

The extension then draws the panel and side panel from the same comment data, in plain words and text only, as Pipeup does. Safari has no side panel, so there the comments open in Pipeup's own panel on the page, as today.

5. The spec change

  • With the extension, Pipeup's menu is the toolbar panel: where comments go, who you are, Copy comments (As Markdown, As text), Theme, Comments, Hide comments and Start commenting, then Always on for this site and Turn off on this page.
  • The comments are in the browser's side panel (Chrome and Edge's side panel, Firefox's sidebar): the list with @ For you and ✓ Resolved, every thread with its replies and actions, and the box for a new comment, which says what it's on. It stays open as the reviewer moves around the page.
  • The page shows only where comments are (highlights and pins) and, in comment mode, what is about to be chosen. Clicking a highlight opens its thread in the side panel; choosing a thread there scrolls the page to it.
  • Where the browser has no side panel (Safari), the comments open in Pipeup's own panel on the page.

6. Decisions (agreed 9 November 2026)

Comments in the side panel? Agreed: yes. It stays open, has room for threads and doesn't cover the page. The other choice is the toolbar panel, which closes on every click outside it, so it can't hold a conversation.

Add-ons in the extension. Agreed: their menu rows move into the toolbar panel when the add-ons come to the extension, a later step. Until then, the extension runs plain Pipeup.

Order of work. Agreed: first the toolbar panel as the full menu (no Pipeup change needed), then the side panel, which needs Pipeup's "drawn by the host" way of running.

What changes, in one place

  • Pipeup's menu, All comments, threads and the comment box move off the page into the browser's toolbar panel and side panel.
  • The page keeps highlights, pins and comment mode's outline.
  • Pipeup gains a way of running where a host draws its interface; pages with Pipeup built in are unchanged.
