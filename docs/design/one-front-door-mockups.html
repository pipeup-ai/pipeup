One front door

The home page goes back to one way in. Instead of two buttons that ask visitors to pick a role, the first screen shows the whole round trip in four steps, with one Copy prompt button in the first. The reviewer page stays, as help that authors link to rather than a door on the home page.

What changes

  • The two buttons become four numbered steps on the first screen: copy the prompt, paste it into your AI and save the file it gives back, send that file with a note, paste the comments you get back into your AI. Copy prompt is the only main button.
  • The sentence under the headline says where comments live: each person's comments stay in their own browser until they send them to you.
  • Quick start becomes “What your AI does”: what the prompt asks for, the prompt in full, and a note for reviewers with its own Copy, for when the AI doesn't write one.
  • A new “The details” section for people adding Pipeup themselves: the two lines to paste, what it sends, what it does to the page, the licence, version and size.
  • add-comments.html becomes the reviewer help page, linked from step 3 and from the note. create-html.html is retired: its address opens the home page's steps.
  • The prompt asks the AI for more: the whole new file when it can't edit yours, a reminder to open it in a browser, and the reviewer note word for word.

Why: in the study, four of seven people took the wrong door first (I2). Nobody saw how comments come back (I1, I3), and chat users weren't told to open the file in a browser or save the file their AI hands back (I15, I20). A design panel then drew up four one-door concepts, and five people from the study scored them (end of this page).

Under the first screen: What your AI does

This replaces Quick start, whose steps are now on the first screen. It shows the prompt in full so people can read it before copying, explains the key in one sentence, says how to check the new file, and gives a note for reviewers that works even when the AI skips it.

What your AI does

The prompt asks your AI to add one line to your HTML file, and a key that keeps this page's comments apart from your other pages. The key isn't tied to an account: it travels with the page, so anyone who has the page has it. Nothing else on the page changes, and there is nothing to install. If your AI is making the page, add the prompt to your request.

Your AI gives you back the new file to save, and a note to send with it. Open the new file in your web browser before you send it: a comment button in the bottom-right corner means it worked.

If your AI didn't write the note, send this one:

A note for your reviewers Copy
You can comment right on this page. Open the file in a web browser such as Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox; a preview inside email, chat or a drive may not show the comments. Click the comment button in the bottom-right corner and choose Start commenting, then click anything, or select some words, and type. On a phone, tap instead of click. When you're done, click the comment button again, choose Copy comments › As Markdown and paste it into a reply to me. Your comments stay in your browser until you send them. Help: pipeup-ai.github.io/pipeup/add-comments.html

Each person sends you their own comments, and nobody sees anyone else's. Paste them into the same chat and your AI makes the changes. Gathering everyone's comments in one place needs a service to hold them: see Share.

The prompt Copy
Add Pipeup to this HTML page (or the one you are making) so the people I share it with can comment on it right on the page.

1. Add <script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/pipeup@0.8.2/dist/pipeup.min.js" integrity="sha384-…" crossorigin="anonymous"></script> just before </body>. Edit only the HTML file: there is nothing to download or run.
2. Add data-pipeup-doc="Xq3…:9fK…" to the <html> element, and keep it exactly as it is from then on. This key is for this one page: every other page needs a key of its own, never a copy of this one, or their comments mix.
3. Mark stable blocks (sections, figures, cards) with short data-pipeup-id names, label charts and images with data-pipeup-label, and mark navigation with data-pipeup-ignore.
4. Don't change the page's layout to make room for Pipeup.

When you're done:
- If you can't change the file on my computer, give me the whole new file to download, and remind me to share that file, not the one I had before.
- Tell me to open it in my web browser and check for the comment button in the bottom-right corner.
- Give me this note to send with it, word for word: "You can comment right on this page. …"

Later I will paste the comments people send me. Apply each one to the page, tell me what you changed, and give me the whole file again.
The full guide: https://pipeup-ai.github.io/pipeup/skills/pipeup-integrate/SKILL.md

The details

What Pipeup sends, what it does to your page, and how to add it without an AI.

Adding it by hand. Put the two lines in the box on your page. The script line alone starts Pipeup; the key keeps the page's comments with it when the file is moved or renamed, and each page needs its own. Readers' browsers load the file from jsDelivr, pinned to this version by its integrity hash, or you can keep a copy of pipeup.min.js next to your page.

What it sends. Nothing. Comments stay in each reviewer's browser and leave only when they copy and send them. Clearing browser data, or closing a private window, removes comments not yet sent. Add-ons that send anything say what, and to whom, on their own pages.

What it does to your page. It adds one element of its own, with everything it draws inside it, and never restyles or moves your page. It works on pages a framework renders and keeps up when a single-page app changes route.

Using Claude Code or Codex? Install the skills once and “add comments to this page” is enough: npx --yes pipeup@0 skills install says what it will write and asks first; skills remove takes them out.

Licence and version. Free and open source under the MIT licence; source and releases are on GitHub. Version 0.8.2 (what changed): one file of about 60 KB compressed, with no dependencies.

The spec wording

As written into WEBSITE_SPEC §3 (in short here; the spec has it in full). Items 1 and 2 are replaced, 2a is new, and item 3 shrinks to the reviewer help page. Functional requirements only. green is new.

1. First screen: the headline “Feedback for any HTML.”, one sentence on the value that also says each person's comments stay in their own browser until they send them, and the showcase beside them (tops aligned).

Under the sentence, four numbered steps take an author from their HTML file to their reviewers and back: copy the prompt; paste it into an AI (Claude, ChatGPT or any chat) with the HTML file, and save the new file it gives back; send that file with the note the AI writes, and people open it in a web browser, comment, and send their comments back; paste their comments into the AI, which makes the changes. Each step is at most two lines on a computer, and the first screen still fits a 1280 × 800 window. The first step holds the page's one main button, Copy prompt, with “one copy per page” and a link that eases down to the prompt's words. The third step ends with a quiet link to the reviewer help page. The first screen has no other button and no choice of paths.

Copy prompt copies exactly the words shown under What your AI does, with a fresh document key made in the visitor's browser; every copy carries a key of its own, for one page. Its icon becomes a check and its label “Copied” for two seconds, then it eases back, and screen readers hear that the prompt was copied. If copying isn't allowed, the page eases down to the prompt and selects its words, and the button doesn't say “Copied”.

Under the steps is a line to try it on this page: the comment shortcut, shown the way the visitor's keyboard labels it; on a touch screen, the comment button in the bottom-right corner, then Start commenting, then a tap on anything. Under that, a quiet line for people adding Pipeup themselves leads to The details.

At phone width the order is: headline, sentence, the try line, the showcase, then the steps, with Copy prompt at full width. Reading and keyboard order follow what is shown, and nothing scrolls sideways. The page's Markdown copy and llms.txt say the same in words.

The showcase: as now, plus: only the scene on show reaches a screen reader, as one sentence saying what happens in it. It stops moving on its own while keyboard focus or the pointer is on it, and while the visitor is commenting on the home page. On a touch screen its pointer is drawn as a fingertip. Focus rings on its controls are never cut off.

2. What your AI does, under the first screen, on the same columns. In plain sentences: what the prompt asks for (one line in the HTML file, and a key that keeps this page's comments apart, isn't tied to an account and travels with the page; nothing else changes and nothing is installed); that the AI gives back a new file to save and a note to send with it, and that the author opens the new file in a web browser first, where a comment button in the bottom-right corner means it worked; a note for reviewers in a labelled box with its own Copy, for when the AI didn't write one (open the file in a web browser, since a preview may not show the comments; turn on commenting from the comment button; comment; Copy comments › As Markdown into a reply; comments stay in the browser until sent; a link to the reviewer help page); and that each person sends their own comments, nobody sees anyone else's, and one shared place needs a service, with a link to Share. Beside them, the prompt in a labelled box, exactly the words Copy prompt copies, with its own Copy that behaves the same way. The prompt asks the AI to add the pinned script with its integrity hash and this copy's key; to mark stable blocks and leave the layout alone; when it can't change the author's file, to hand back the whole new file and say to share that one; to tell the author to open it in a web browser and check for the comment button; to give the reviewer note word for word; and later to apply each pasted comment, say what changed, and give the whole file again.

2a. The details, the last section above the footer, on the same columns: one sentence, then short paragraphs led by a few bold words, each saying its drawbacks too, never a facts row, chips or tiles. Adding it by hand (the key and the script line; the script alone starts Pipeup; the key keeps comments with the page when it moves, one per page; jsDelivr with its integrity hash, or a copy next to the page). What it sends (nothing; unsent comments go when browser data is cleared). What it does to the page (one element of its own; never restyles or moves the page; works with frameworks and route changes). The agent skills (install once, asks first, how to remove). Licence and version (MIT, free, GitHub, the version with what changed, its size, no dependencies), always matching the copy of Pipeup the site carries. Beside them, a For your page box with the two lines and its own Copy (a fresh key every copy).

3. The reviewer help page, add-comments.html, for people who were sent a page, linked from step 3, the note and the reviewer prompt: four steps (open it in a web browser; turn on commenting; comment; send them back with Copy comments › As Markdown in a reply), in tap words on a touch screen; a line saying comments stay in this browser until sent and go if browser data is cleared; and a quiet fallback for a page with no comment button, which shows the reviewer prompt or the script line in full before copying. Three decisions, as decided

1. Show the version, size and licence again?

They were taken off on 15 October: the size had gone out of date and the facts line read as marketing. In the study, Theo and Lee both stalled without them I14.

Decided: yes, as one sentence in The details, filled in from the copy of Pipeup the site carries, with a site check that fails if the two differ.

2. Retire create-html.html, or keep it as a short page?

Keeping it gives authors a second place to start, which is the split this change removes. Retiring it keeps one door, and its address still lands on the steps.

Decided: retire it, with the address opening the home page's steps.

3. Keep “Copy comments › As Markdown” in the reviewer note?

As Markdown opens with instructions for coding agents, which reviewers see in their reply I7. As text reads well, but drops where each comment is, which the author's AI needs.

Decided: keep As Markdown, and in the same release rewrite its header in the library so it reads to a person and still guides an AI (a library change, spec first, for next).
