Copy comments › As Markdown, readable by a person

Reviewers send their comments back to the author with Copy comments › As Markdown, pasted into a reply. Today that reply opens with a timestamp and a paragraph for coding agents (usability study, issue I7). This proposal makes it read as a message to the author, while an AI still gets everything it needs.

What changes

  • The opening is for a person. "Comments on Q3 launch plan", then how many open comments there are, who wrote them and when they were copied, in words: 3 open comments from Lee, copied 27 Oct 2026, 09:40 UTC. Then the page's address. The ISO timestamp and the paragraph for agents are gone from the top.
  • Each thread leads with where it is, as its heading, then the quoted words or the pin in a sentence ("On the words "20% lift".") and the comments.
  • What only an AI needs moves to one last line per thread: open, addressed or resolved; the thread's id; and the element's data-pipeup-id, saying whether the comment is on it or inside it.
  • Replies carry no ids. No command or skill uses them. They stay in Pipeup.comments() and pipeup read --json.
  • The instruction for an AI comes after the threads, marked "For an AI assistant:", and now names the skill that says how to answer.

The spec's words (§7a)

  • Copying a thread or copying all produces text a person can read in an email or chat reply, and an AI assistant can act on directly. Reviewers send it back to the author (§11); authors paste it into an AI.
  • Copy comments › As Markdown and a thread's own copy button give Markdown:
    • Copying all opens with what a person needs, and nothing aimed at an AI: the page's title ("Comments on …"); how many open comments there are and who wrote them; when they were copied, as a date and a time with its time zone; how many resolved ones are left out; and the page's address.
    • Then, for each thread, a numbered heading saying where it is (slide or section, then the element); the words it quotes, or where it is pinned, in a sentence; the comment and its replies as a list, each with who and how long before copying; and, when an agent marked it addressed, who did and their note.
    • Each thread ends with one line for an AI: whether it is open, addressed or resolved; the thread's id, which an agent needs to answer it; and the element's stable identifier, saying whether the comment is on that element or inside it. These are the only ids in the text.
    • After the threads, one paragraph, marked as being for an AI assistant, says to work through the open comments in order, say what changed or why not, answer each in its thread with Pipeup (naming the skill that says how) and mark it addressed, and never resolve it: the author does.
    • A thread's own copy has its heading (without a number), the page's address and the same lines, without the opening or the closing paragraph.
  • Unchanged: As text, Copy for my agent's own words, the menu's tooltips, and the data that Pipeup.comments() and pipeup read --json return.

Decisions for the maintainer

1. Where does the instruction for an AI go?

At the top, a person reads it first. At the end, they read the comments first and can stop before it.

Recommended: at the end, after a rule, starting "For an AI assistant:". An AI reads the whole paste either way.

2. Drop the comment ids on replies?

No command, skill or reply block uses them: an agent answers a thread by the thread's id.

Recommended: drop them from the text; they stay in the data.

3. Keep times as "22m ago"?

They count back from when the comments were copied, which the opening line now says in words. Clock times would need a time zone on every line.

Recommended: keep them, unchanged.
