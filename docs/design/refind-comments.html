Re-finding comments when a site changes

Websites change all the time. A comment should land on the right thing when it can, say so plainly when it isn't sure, and never vanish when its place is gone. This adds more clues to each comment, a question when Pipeup isn't sure, a way to point a comment at its place again, and a clearer record of what a lost comment was about.

2. Remember more than a position

When someone comments on a thing they clicked, Pipeup also keeps a few clues about it. Each is small text, kept with the comment and sent with it:

  • What kind of thing it is: a heading, a paragraph, a picture, a button, a link, a box.
  • Its words: the start of its text, as today.
  • Its name, where it has one: a button's or link's label, a picture's description or file name, a link's address.
  • The heading it sits under, such as "Pricing".
  • Roughly where it was down the page.

Text comments already keep their words and the words around them; they gain the heading they sit under too.

Lost

One change to step 2: today an old position always wins, even when what's there now has different words. In future, if something elsewhere matches the clues clearly better, the comment goes there instead. That is the Free-tier case above: the Team box still says "Team" and "$20 a month", so the comment follows it.

A close call between two look-alikes (two "Learn more" buttons, say) is never guessed: the comment is Lost, so nobody reads it against the wrong thing.

4. When Pipeup isn't sure, it asks

A comment that was re-found by looking alike says so on its card, with one question. Anyone reading can answer, and the answer is kept for everyone, so it's asked only once.

Not here lets the reader show the right place, or say it's gone:

Shown at the top of the page while picking, with Esc to cancel. Picking works as commenting does: hover outlines, a click chooses. "It's gone" makes it Lost on purpose, so nobody is asked again.

7. The spec change

Written into §8, Staying attached on changing pages, in docs/FUNCTIONAL_SPEC.md; the picture goes in docs/EXTENSION_SPEC.md.

  • A comment remembers enough about what it was on to find it again after the page changes: its words, what kind of thing it is, its name where it has one (a label, a picture's description, a link's address), the heading it sits under, and roughly where it was.
  • A comment never stays on something that no longer matches it when something else on the page clearly does. When two things match about equally, it is Lost rather than guessed.
  • A comment found by looking alike is Moved and asks Is this the right place? Anyone reading can answer Yes or Not here; the answer is kept for everyone. Not here lets them show the right place, or say it's gone.
  • A Lost comment (today's Orphaned) stays in All comments and says what it was: the kind of thing, the headings it sat under and its words. Anyone can show where it now belongs.
  • Copy for AI describes Moved and Lost comments the same way, so an agent can find their place in the page's source.
  • Extension spec: the extension keeps a small picture of where each comment was made, in that browser only. It is never sent, shared or copied, and the reviewer can turn pictures off.

8. Decisions (agreed 9 November 2026)

Who can answer "Is this the right place?" Agreed: anyone reading, kept for everyone, and shown in the thread as "Ada confirmed the place". The other choice is only the commenter, which leaves the question open when they've moved on.

Pictures in the extension. Agreed: on by default, this browser only. The other choice is off by default.

Rename Orphaned to Lost in the spec, to match the words people see. Agreed.

What changes, in one place

  • Comments on things people click follow them when a site adds or reorders content, instead of landing on a neighbour.
  • Pipeup asks once when it isn't sure, and anyone can point a comment at its place again.
  • Lost comments say what and where they were, for people and agents.
  • With the extension, a small private picture of where each comment was made.
  • Unchanged: text comments' matching, editions, views and slides.
