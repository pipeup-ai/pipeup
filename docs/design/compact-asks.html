Compact inline asks

The inline add-on's asks are too big for the content they sit in, and after answering they take four rows. This proposal makes compact the default, makes it obvious where to type, and gives agents what they need to match the page, including their own Commenting toggle and Pipeup's menus. Pipeup's mark and a light tint always stay, so a reviewer can still see where to answer.

Version 3: the menu opens in its usual place; Version 2: input fields have no resize handle and say what to type; customising is written for agents; new section 6 on a page's own Commenting toggle and menus.

Today, after answering, an ask shows four rows: the question, "Your answer: Yes", Change and Remove, and "1 comment · latest from you". 3. Writing words: clearly a field, no resize handle

An open question, Needs change, Agree's optional words and Other all use the same field. It has to look like somewhere to type, at a glance:

  • A white field with a visible edge on the tint, and words inside it that say what to type: "Type your answer", "What needs to change?", "Anything to add? (optional)".
  • No resize handle. The field starts as one line and grows on its own as the reviewer writes, up to about five lines, then scrolls.
  • Send sits inside the field, faded until there are words, then in the accent. Return sends; Shift + Return starts a new line.
  • Pointing at the field or focusing it darkens its edge, so it answers before anything is typed.

5. For agents: matching the page, staying obvious

The defaults above are what every page gets. We've seen agents restyle asks to match the page they're building, which is fine; the add-on's documentation and prompt tell them how, with one rule: an ask always looks like something to answer. An agent can set the ask's accent colour, its font and how rounded its corners are, from the page's own stylesheet. Pipeup's mark before the question and a light tint of the accent behind the ask always stay.

6. For agents: their own Commenting toggle and Pipeup's menus

Some pages want reviewing to feel like part of the page: a Comment button in their own header instead of Pipeup's control in the corner.

Already possible: their own Commenting toggle

A page's own code can turn comment mode on and off, ask whether it's on, hear each time it changes (however it changed), and set the reviewer's name (spec §4a). Agents aren't told today. The prompt and the skill will say so, with a short example: a Comment button in the page's header that turns comment mode on and off and shows when it's on.

Launch planComment⋯

The page's own Comment button turns comment mode on and off. Its own ⋯ opens Pipeup's menu.

New: hiding Pipeup's control, opening its menu from the page

Today Pipeup's comment control always shows in the corner, and its menu (All comments, Copy comments, Hide comments, the add-ons' rows) opens only from it. Proposed:

  • A page with its own toggle can hide Pipeup's comment control. The shortcut keeps working, and so does everything else.
  • The page's own code can open Pipeup's menu (in its usual place) and open All comments.
  • A page that hides the control takes on giving reviewers a way to its menu: without the menu, a reviewer can't copy or send their comments. The skill says so, and pipeup check warns when the control is hidden and nothing on the page opens the menu.
  • Which rows the menu has stays Pipeup's and the add-ons': a page can't remove Copy comments or Hide comments.

7. The prompt and the skill

The inline add-on's Copy prompt on the Add-ons page, its Markdown copy, llms.txt and the integrate skill gain one step (shaded).

1. Add this script tag just after the Pipeup script tag, before </body>: …/@pipeup/inline@…/dist/inline.min.js
2. Mark each question with data-pipeup-id, data-pipeup-ask and data-pipeup-ask-kind; inside, write the question for anyone without the add-on.
3. Asks take the page's font. To match the page's look, set the ask's accent colour, font and corner rounding with the settings the add-on documents. Don't hide or rebuild the ask's own controls, and keep Pipeup's mark and tint, so reviewers can see where to answer.
…

The integrate skill, llms.txt and the Create prompt's guide also gain a short section for any page, with or without add-ons:

If the page should have its own Commenting button, use Pipeup's page API: turn comment mode on and off from your button, show when it's on, and listen for changes. You can hide Pipeup's comment control, but then give reviewers your own way to open Pipeup's menu (Copy comments lives there), and open All comments from your page if you like.

8. The spec change

These replace or add to bullets in §11b of the functional spec, under Asks, and add to §4a.

Compact. An ask takes as little room as its question needs. The question and its buttons share one line when they fit, and wrap under the question when they don't. Touch screens keep finger-sized targets.

Where to type is obvious. Where words are asked for, the ask has one field that looks like a field at a glance and says what to type. It starts as a single line and grows on its own as the reviewer writes; it has no handle for resizing. Its Send is inside it and works once there are words.

After answering, the ask is one line: the question, the reviewer's answer ("You said Yes"), Change and Remove. A second line appears only when there is more to see: how many answered and the count for each option, and any replies. When the only comment is the reviewer's own answer, nothing more is shown. Remove's second question and Change's Cancel stay on the ask's line.

The page's look. An ask's question takes the page's font. The author can set the ask's accent colour, font and corner rounding from the page's own styles. Whatever they set, an ask always shows Pipeup's mark and a light tint of its accent, so reviewers can tell it is something to answer, and its controls keep working the same way.

§4a, added. A page with its own way to start commenting can hide Pipeup's comment control; the shortcut and everything else keep working. Its code can open Pipeup's menu and All comments. What the menu holds stays Pipeup's and its add-ons'. Pipeup's checks warn when the control is hidden and nothing on the page opens the menu.

What changes, in one place

  • Asks are compact: one line for a question and its buttons where it fits, a single-line field with Send inside it.
  • After sending, one line ("You said Yes · Change · Remove"), plus one quiet line only for others' answers and replies.
  • The question takes the page's font; the author can match the accent colour, font and rounding; the mark and tint always stay.
  • Fields look like fields, say what to type, grow on their own and have no resize handle.
  • The inline prompt, llms.txt and the integrate skill tell agents how to match the page without hiding what's askable.
  • Agents are told they can give the page its own Commenting toggle (possible today); new: a page can hide Pipeup's control and open its menu and All comments from its own buttons.
  • Touch sizes, keyboard and screen-reader behaviour, the dark look and "nobody is steered" are unchanged.
