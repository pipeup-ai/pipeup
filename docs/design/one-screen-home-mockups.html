PipeupGitHub

What your AI does.

The prompt asks it to add Pipeup to your HTML file and change nothing else. There is nothing to install.

  • It adds one script line, and a key that keeps this page's comments apart from your other pages.
  • It gives you back the whole file. Open it in your web browser: a comment button in the bottom-right corner means it worked.
  • It writes a note for your reviewers. If it didn't, send this one:
    A note for your reviewers Copy
    You can comment right on this page. Open the file in a web browser such as Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox; a preview inside email, chat or a drive may not show the comments. Click the comment button in the bottom-right corner and choose Start commenting, then click anything… Help
  • When you paste the comments people send you, it makes the changes and gives you the file again.

Each person sends you their own comments. To gather everyone's in one place, see Share.

The prompt Copy prompt
Add Pipeup to this HTML page (or the one you are making) so the people I share it with can comment on it right on the page.

1. Add <script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/pipeup@0.8.2/dist/pipeup.min.js" integrity="sha384-…" crossorigin="anonymous"></script> just before </body>. Edit only the HTML file: there is nothing to download or run.
2. Add data-pipeup-doc="Xq3…:9fK…" to the <html> element, and keep it exactly as it is from then on. This key is for this one page: every other page needs a key of its own…
3. Mark stable blocks (sections, figures, cards) with short data-pipeup-id names, label charts and images with data-pipeup-label, and mark navigation with data-pipeup-ignore.
4. Don't change the page's layout to make room for Pipeup.

When you're done:
- If you can't change the file on my computer, give me the whole new file to download, and remind me to share that file, not the one I had before.
- Tell me to open it in my web browser and check for the comment button in the bottom-right corner.
- Give me this note to send with it, word for word: "You can comment right on this page. …"

Later I will paste the comments people send me. Apply each one to the page, tell me what you changed, and give me the whole file again.

PipeupGitHub

The details.

For adding Pipeup yourself, and what it does to your page.

By hand. Put the two lines on your page; each page needs its own key. Browsers load Pipeup from jsDelivr, pinned by its integrity hash, or from a copy you keep next to the page.

What it sends. Nothing. Comments stay in each reviewer's browser until they send them, so clearing browser data removes the ones not yet sent.

What it does to your page. It adds one element of its own and never restyles or moves your page, including pages a framework renders.

Claude Code or Codex. Run npx --yes pipeup@0 skills install once (it asks first), then say “add comments to this page”.

Licence and version. Free and open source under the MIT licence, on GitHub. Version 0.8.2 (what changed), one file of about 60 KB compressed, with no dependencies.

The spec wording

As it would go into WEBSITE_SPEC §3, in short. Functional requirements only.

1. First screen: as now, with shorter steps, and the home page is that one screen: the footer follows it, with nothing else to scroll to. The first step's quiet link eases down to the prompt's words opens What your AI does; the quiet line for people adding Pipeup themselves opens The details.

2. What your AI does, a page of its own (prompt.html) with the site's header and footer and its own Markdown copy: one sentence, four short lines on what the prompt asks (one line and a key; the whole file back, opened in a browser to check; a note for reviewers, with its own Copy, for when the AI didn't write one; later, the changes), a line on each person sending their own comments with a link to Share, and the prompt in full with its own Copy.

2a. The details, a page of its own (details.html) with the site's header and footer and its own Markdown copy: one sentence, then five paragraphs of one or two sentences each, led by a few bold words and each saying its drawback; beside them the For your page box with its Copy.

Change log: 2026-10-27 — The home page fits one screen: What your AI does and The details become pages of their own, opened from the first screen's quiet links, and the first screen's words get shorter.

Two questions

1. The two pages' addresses

prompt.html and details.html are short and say what's on them. The other choice is what-your-ai-does.html, which matches the heading but is long to type or say.

Recommended: prompt.html and details.html.

2. Keep “One copy per page” on the first screen?

It's four words, and the only first-screen text that isn't a step. Every copy now carries its own key, but someone who pastes one copy into two pages still mixes their comments. That was a real failure in the study.

Recommended: keep it, beside the button.
